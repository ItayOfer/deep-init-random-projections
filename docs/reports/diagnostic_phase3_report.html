Key Findings

1. The plateau lesson generalises. Two of the three target architectures (α and γ) were pushed past the thesis criterion just by adding ReduceLROnPlateau to recipes that previously hit ceilings at ~0.985 (α) or collapsed (γ). The mechanism is consistent: each LR drop arrives precisely when learning stalls, restarts the climb, and (crucially for BN-containing runs) prevents the BN running statistics from accumulating destabilising drift.
2. fmnist/50L/BN is uniquely resistant. Run β didn't collapse — bn_var stayed bounded at 5.13 (vs 29 in Phase 2 B's runaway) — but it never reached the threshold either. The loss trajectory is oscillatory: spikes at ep 17 (loss = 1.7) and ep 41 (loss = 1.16) suggest mini-instabilities that the scheduler reacts to (by dropping LR) but doesn't fully resolve. The eventual plateau sits at acc ≈ 0.95, loss ≈ 0.17 — close to but not at pass.
3. Plateau hurt cifar10/100L/BN (δ₁) and barely helped fmnist/100L/BN (δ₂). The asymmetry is informative: δ₁ stayed at 0.11 throughout because the scheduler saw the very-slow-learning phase as a plateau and ratcheted LR to 10⁻⁶ before the model had time to escape. δ₂ went the same direction LR-wise, but on fmnist that low LR (~3×10⁻⁵) was apparently enough to inch the model into a late breakthrough (0.10 → 0.34 across ep 60–100). 100L FC with He init appears to be on the very edge of trainability with BN; cifar10's higher input dimensionality (3072 vs 784) seems to put it just over the edge.
4. The mid-run wobble in Phase 2 A was real — and plateau caught it. Phase 2 A (SGD const lr=0.003) showed a dip at ep 61–71 (acc 0.98 → 0.80 → recovery). Phase 3 α (same recipe + plateau) shows no such dip; the scheduler started dropping LR around ep 57, which kept the climb monotonic all the way to 0.9997.
5. The thesis story now has a clean structure:
  1. Depth 30 (any BN, any dataset): trivially trainable with He init out of the box.
  2. Depth 50 with NoBN: trainable with constant LR + plateau adaptive drops.
  3. Depth 50 with BN: requires adaptive LR (plateau) to prevent BN-statistic runaway; cifar10 passes cleanly, fmnist gets close.
  4. Depth 100 with NoBN: unconditionally dead — gradients vanish to zero within 3 epochs, both datasets.
  5. Depth 100 with BN: borderline-untrainable — neither cosine nor plateau pushes acc past ~0.34. He init is insufficient at this depth even with normalisation.

Optional Phase 4 (only if you want to chase the remaining cases)

Worth doing — small, focused experiments:
  • β extended. Re-run β with longer plateau patience (e.g. patience=10, factor=0.7) and 200 epochs. The loss is already close to 0.17 with bn_var stable — a gentler schedule may push it to ≤ 0.10.
  • 100L/BN with warmup. The δ₁ failure was the scheduler firing too early. Try a constant LR for the first 20 epochs (warmup), then enable plateau. If 100L can still be saved, this is how.
  • Document the negative results. Even if Phase 4 doesn't add new passes, the thesis already has clean statements: 100L/NoBN dead (both datasets), 100L/BN borderline-untrainable, fmnist/50L/BN trainable but not to threshold under the explored recipes.
Stopping point candidate. Phase 3 produced two new clean passes and a clear taxonomy of the remaining failures. The marginal value of further tuning runs is now low: the 5 remaining architectures fall into "needs more careful tuning" (β) or "needs an architectural fix beyond initialisation" (the 100L cases). A natural next step is to write up rather than continue tuning.
