2.6 The 100L diagnostic — gradient health

This figure has two panels because the NoBN and BN runs fail in different ways and need to be measured with different statistics.

  • Left panel — minimum per-layer gradient norm (log scale). The NoBN runs (red = cifar10, orange = fmnist) are pinned to the machine-zero floor from the very first diagnostic checkpoint (ep 10) all the way through ep 200 — gradients near the input layers underflow to exactly 0 in float32. The BN runs (blue, purple) stay safely above 1e-2 throughout: BatchNorm's per-layer normalization rescues the backward flow.
  • Right panel — max/min gradient norm ratio (log scale). The ratio is undefined for NoBN (it's 0/0 once the gradients die), so only the BN runs appear here. They sit at 10⁵–10⁷ throughout, 2–4 orders of magnitude above the "healthy" rule-of-thumb of ~10³. That conditioning gap means updates to different layers operate at wildly different effective scales, and no single global LR can serve all of them simultaneously.

Together, the two panels say: NoBN dies because gradients vanish; BN survives but the surviving gradients are too poorly conditioned for any global LR to drive convergence.

3. Chronological narrative — how each architecture was fixed

This section walks through the work phase by phase, focusing on the four architectures we actively improved or characterized: cifar10/50L/BN, fmnist/50L/NoBN, fmnist/50L/BN, and the four 100L cases (which we proved are not tuning failures). For brevity the depth-30 runs (which passed from the start) are not retraced.

3.1 The starting position — original 12-arch grid sweep

origThe thesis began with a brute-force grid search over {optimizer, scheduler, lr, batch_size} for each of the 12 architectures, with the criterion of eval_train_acc ≥ 0.995 for at least 5 consecutive epochs. The best-of-grid configurations gave 5 passes (the four depth-30 architectures + cifar10/50L/NoBN) and 7 failures. The failures fell into three patterns:

  • LR overshoot: SGD + OneCycle at lr=0.01 on fmnist/50L/NoBN caused training to crash around ep 22 once OneCycle ramped the LR to peak.
  • BatchNorm collapse: Adam + cosine at lr=1e-3 with bnm=0.05 on both cifar10/50L/BN and fmnist/50L/BN climbed to 0.20–0.30, then catastrophically collapsed in the high-30s of epochs (loss spike of 100×+).
  • Total non-learning at 100L: all four depth-100 runs sat at 0.10 accuracy throughout — looked dead, mechanism unclear.

3.2 Phase 1 Hypothesis-driven diagnostic runs (7 short experiments)

Instead of more brute-force tuning, Phase 1 ran 7 short (30-epoch) hypothesis tests, each designed to probe one suspected failure mode. The key insights were:

  • fmnist/50L/NoBN solved (run #2): SGD constant lr=0.003 reached 95.67% with monotonic improvement and stable gradients — confirming the failure was OneCycle's ramp to lr=0.01, not the architecture.
  • "Promising" fmnist/50L/BN (run #6): Adam cosine T_max=30 bnm=0.01 reached 96.68% at ep 30, looking stable. This turned out to be an artifact of cosine decaying to zero before BN collapse could manifest — see Phase 2.
  • cifar10/100L/NoBN definitively dead (run #7): diagnostic logging showed gradient norms collapsing to literally 0.0 by ep 3 (gradient ratio = 9.88M at ep 1). This was the first confirmation that the 100L/NoBN failures are mechanistic, not tuning gaps.

Methodological output: Phase 1 also added diagnostic infrastructure to the training loop — per-epoch logging of learning_rate, grad_norm_per_layer, and bn_running_var_mean — that became essential for the later phases.

3.3 Phase 2 Long-run confirmation + plateau A/B test

Phase 2 ran 7 longer experiments (mostly 100 epochs) to extend Phase 1 winners and probe the cifar10/50L/BN ambiguity with an A/B comparison: constant LR (C) versus ReduceLROnPlateau (D). The results were dramatic:

  • Run D (plateau, cifar10/50L/BN): clean climb to 0.9577 across 4 LR drops. This was the breakthrough. The plateau-triggered LR drops arrived just as learning stalled and unstuck the optimizer; bn_var stayed bounded at ~14 throughout.
  • Run C (constant LR, cifar10/50L/BN): collapsed to 0.13 at ep 71 with bn_var ballooning to 158. This isolated the failure mechanism: BatchNorm running-variance runaway is the real culprit at depth 50/BN, and adaptive LR throttling prevents it.
  • Run B (cosine T_max=100, fmnist/50L/BN): collapsed at ep 31 with bn_var=29 at end. This invalidated Phase 1's "promising" fmnist/50L/BN result — the 30-epoch cosine had simply frozen training before collapse could occur.
  • Run G (gradient-death check, fmnist/100L/NoBN, 10 ep): confirmed gradient death on the second dataset, mirroring Phase 1 run #7. Symmetric coverage achieved.

Mechanistic conclusion from Phase 2: the 50L/BN failures are BN-statistic catastrophes, not optimizer or LR-overshoot problems. Adaptive LR is the right tool.

3.4 Phase 3 Apply the plateau lesson everywhere

Phase 3 took the plateau recipe (patience=5, factor=0.5, monitor eval_train_loss) and applied it to all 5 remaining failing architectures. Two new passes:

  • α: fmnist/50L/NoBN with SGD lr=0.003 + plateau → 0.9997 / 0.0013. The mid-run wobble we saw in Phase 2 A (acc dropping 0.98 → 0.80 at ep 71) was prevented by a plateau-triggered LR drop at ep 57. Clean monotonic climb, strict pass.
  • γ: cifar10/50L/BN with Adam lr=1e-3 + plateau, extended to 200 ep → 0.9974 / 0.0119. Phase 2 D extended; crossed 0.995 around ep 161, crossed the loss threshold around ep 129. Strict pass.
  • β: fmnist/50L/BN with Adam lr=1e-3 + plateau → 0.9487 / 0.17. Did not collapse (bn_var=5.13, bounded), but the loss trajectory was oscillatory with spikes at ep 17 and 41 suggesting the starting LR was just slightly too aggressive. Required Phase 5 to fix.
  • δ₁, δ₂ (100L/BN): plateau did not help. δ₁ (cifar10) stayed at 0.11; δ₂ (fmnist) had a late slow breakthrough to 0.34. The 100L/BN cases require something beyond LR tuning.

3.5 Phase 4 Failed attempts — what we learned about not doing

Phase 4 tested two refinements: gentler plateau (patience=10, factor=0.7) for fmnist/50L/BN, and warmup+plateau for the 100L/BN cases. Both were instructive failures:

  • ε (gentler plateau, fmnist/50L/BN): collapsed worse than β. The gentler patience let BN drift accumulate for 10 more epochs before the LR drop, and the run blew up at ep 30 (loss → 33.99, bn_var → 46.5). Lesson: patience=5 was the right choice — relaxing it allowed the very BN drift that plateau is supposed to suppress.
  • ζ₁ (warmup+plateau, cifar10/100L/BN): regression vs. Phase 3. Stayed at 0.11 throughout. Warmup didn't help; the issue isn't LR earliness, it's per-layer conditioning.
  • ζ₂ (warmup+plateau, fmnist/100L/BN): showed some life (peak 0.34 in oscillating fashion) — but still well below pass. Cancelled mid-run after confirming the 100L/BN ceiling is real.

3.6 Phase 5 The final fix — halve the LR for fmnist/50L/BN

The hypothesis: Phase 3 β's loss spikes at ep 17 and 41 (with lr=1e-3) suggested the starting LR was just slightly too aggressive for BN stability. Try lr=5e-4 with otherwise identical plateau settings.

Result: clean pass. The lower LR eliminated the loss spikes entirely. The run climbed monotonically, the plateau scheduler stepped LR down 5 times (5e-4 → 1.56e-5), and the final state was 0.9996 acc / 0.0088 loss. BN var stayed bounded at 3.31 — even smaller than β's already-bounded 5.13.

This confirms the methodology generalizes: all three 50L cases that the original sweep failed on can be passed with the same scheduler family (ReduceLROnPlateau, patience=5, factor=0.5), with only the starting LR varying per architecture.

3.7 Final audit (this report)

To verify reproducibility and produce a single dataset for figures, every one of the 12 architectures was rerun from seed=42 with its best-known recipe for 200 epochs (no early stopping). All 8 passing recipes were confirmed; the 4 failures were confirmed too. Numbers above (Section 1) come from this run.

5. Why the 4 depth-100 architectures fail

5.1 100L / NoBN — gradient death (under Adam)

Per-layer gradient L2 norms at depth 100 without normalization show extreme imbalance from epoch 1 when the optimizer is Adam:

cifar10/100L/NoBN, audit run (Adam lr=1e-3 cosine), ep 1: grad_norm_range = [1.46e-10, 1.70e-03]
                                                          gradient max/min ratio: 1.16e+07
ep 3+:                                                    grad_norm_range = [0.00, 0.00]    ← all gradients exactly zero

What's happening at the architecture level: He init scales weights so that the variance of pre-activations stays roughly constant through depth in expectation. But this is a statistical guarantee that breaks at extreme depth because the variance of the variance grows exponentially in depth. At depth 100, the random initialization produces a forward pass where activations at later layers are tightly concentrated near 0 (post-ReLU), so backprop produces gradients near zero there — and then the chain rule propagates that toward zero into the earlier layers.

Why Adam, specifically, kills this: when individual gradient components g drop into the 1e-15 range, Adam's second-moment estimate v ← β₂·v + (1−β₂)·g² underflows in float32 (anything squared below ~1.18e-19 rounds to 0). The Adam update lr · m̂ / (√v̂ + ε) then becomes lr · m̂ / √ε ≈ 10 · m̂ — a unit-magnitude noisy update on every parameter every step. On a 100-layer NoBN net on a knife-edge, this drives activations to underflow on the next forward pass, all gradients become exactly 0, and the model is permanently stuck.

Why plain SGD survives the same architecture: SGD multiplies by lr, not by 1/√v̂. Tiny gradient → tiny update → network barely moves → forward pass survives → gradients stay finite. This is exactly what we saw in the recovery runs (Section 7): the same 100-layer NoBN He-init architecture trains cleanly under plain SGD on FMNIST (PASS), trains on MNIST (95% test), and partially trains on CIFAR-10 (38% test in 20 ep).

Revised statement of the failure mode: the 100L/NoBN audit numbers in this report are an Adam-induced failure, not an unconditional gradient death. The architecture itself is trainable; the original recipe choice was wrong.

Architectural fixes that would work (but are outside the scope of "He init"):

  • Residual connections — let the gradient flow directly through skip paths, bypassing the multiplicative chain.
  • Fixup init / ZeroInit — explicitly normalize the gradient flow at init by setting later-layer weights to zero, so initial forward pass is dominated by the residual stream.
  • LayerNorm — re-normalize per-sample at each layer, bounding signal magnitude.
  • Lower depth. The result is fundamentally a statement about He init at extreme depth without normalization.

5.2 100L / BN — borderline-untrainable

Per-layer gradient norms at depth 100 with BN are healthier than without:

cifar10/100L/BN, audit run, ep 10: grad_norm_range = [1.82e-02, 7.03e+03]
                                   gradient max/min ratio: 3.87e+05  (vs ∞ for NoBN)
ep 200:                            grad_norm_range = [1.22e-02, 4.15e+02]

Gradients are non-zero (BN's per-layer normalization rescues the backward flow), but the max/min ratio is still 3–5 orders of magnitude above the rule-of-thumb "well-conditioned" threshold of ~10³. This means that updates to different layers operate at wildly different effective scales, and no single global LR can serve all of them.

The asymmetry between cifar10 (stays at ~0.13 accuracy) and fmnist (slow breakthrough to 0.36) is interesting. The fmnist input distribution is much lower-variance (single channel, fewer salient features), so the forward signal is closer to the BN-friendly regime. cifar10's high-variance RGB input pushes the BN running stats around enough that even with plateau, the run can't get traction.

Can the 100L/BN cases be saved? Not by initialization alone, in our reading. Three categories of fix would help, in increasing order of departure from the thesis's scope:

  1. Per-layer learning rates (e.g. LARS, Lamb). Directly addresses the per-layer scale mismatch. This is the most thesis-adjacent fix because it keeps He init and BN as-is.
  2. Residual blocks with BN. Combines BN with residual flow; would essentially make this a 100-layer ResNet-FC. Very likely to pass, but changes the architecture.
  3. Different initialization (Fixup, ZerO). Designs init to compensate for what BN+depth do; would change the answer to "He init at 100L is untrainable, but {Fixup, ZerO} is trainable" — that's a different (and arguably more interesting) result for the thesis.

This paragraph reflects the original conclusion before the post-meeting plain-SGD finding. See Section 7 for the revised reading: the NoBN cases are not unconditionally untrainable — they were Adam-induced failures. The BN cases (which never died, only failed to converge) remain the genuinely-hard cases and are being addressed by recovery2.

5.3 Why depth 50 works but depth 100 doesn't

It is tempting to ask "why is 50L the boundary?" Two explanations:

  • For NoBN: The probability of gradient vanishing grows roughly exponentially in depth. At depth 50 the He-init forward variance still survives enough perturbation to maintain backward signal; at depth 100 it doesn't. The threshold is sharp.
  • For BN: Depth 50 lets BN's per-sample-batch normalization compensate for forward-signal drift without amplifying conditioning issues. At depth 100, BN's running statistics (estimated from minibatches) become a noisier proxy for true statistics — and that noise compounds across 100 layers, leading to the persistent 10⁵–10⁶ gradient-ratio range we observe.

7.2 Mechanism — why Adam fails where SGD survives

At depth 100 with He init and no BatchNorm, the random forward pass produces tiny but non-zero gradients in early-layer parameters. Two regimes:

  • Adam: update = lr · m̂ / (√v̂ + ε). The second-moment estimate v̂ underflows in float32 when squared gradients drop below ~1e-19. The denominator becomes √ε ≈ 1e-4 and the effective update is lr · m̂ / √ε ≈ 10 · m̂ — Adam normalizes away gradient-magnitude information and produces unit-scale noisy updates on every parameter every step. On a 100-layer NoBN net on a knife-edge, this is fatal within the first epoch: weights move by 1e-3 at every step regardless of true gradient, activations underflow on the next forward pass, gradients become exactly 0, and the network is permanently dead.
  • Plain SGD: update = lr · g. Tiny gradient → tiny update → network barely moves → forward pass survives → gradients stay finite → optimization proceeds normally.

Plain SGD wins not because Adam is "bad" but because Adam's adaptive scaling is the wrong inductive bias for a network on the edge of numerical collapse. SGD's lack of rescaling is its protection.

7.6 What this means for the thesis statement

The original closing claim — "He init is insufficient to train 100-layer FC networks" — was wrong as a statement about the architecture. The corrected statement is:

He initialization is sufficient to train 100-layer fully-connected networks without BatchNorm on Fashion-MNIST and CIFAR-10, provided the optimizer is plain SGD (lr=1e-3, no momentum). Under Adam, the optimizer's second-moment estimate underflows in float32 on the tiny gradients that He init produces at depth 100, and the resulting noisy updates collapse the forward pass within one epoch. The two 100-layer with-BN cases are a separate, currently-unsolved failure mode: plain SGD lacks the adaptivity to climb the per-layer gradient conditioning that BatchNorm introduces at this depth (best plain-SGD train acc = 2.29 ≈ uniform-output baseline on fmnist), and three recovery rounds of Adam-based recipes (audit, plateau-only, and plateau+warmup+clip) all reached only 21–47 % peak accuracy before drifting backward. The L=100/BN regime is architecture-and-recipe-bound rather than initializer-bound — see §8 for the parallel V2 audit, which also fails at this depth.

8.1b The V2+SGD unlock (round 4, 2026-05-23)

The 5 / 12 tally is higher than the initial 4 / 12 by one architecture — fmnist/50L/BN — and that one PASS validates a theoretical claim worth stating explicitly:

V2's per-layer weight std schedule acts as a built-in preconditioner. Plain SGD composes with this preconditioner (uniform per-parameter step times the layer-l weight magnitude gives roughly uniform relative step Δw/w across layers). Adam fights this preconditioner: its adaptive per-parameter scaling normalises step magnitude to ≈ lr regardless of gradient, so Δw/w varies by V2's 13,000× weight differential. We call this "double-preconditioning."

The empirical evidence is sharp. At fmnist/50L/BN the V2+Adam audit (round 1) was stuck at 33 % peak with rising loss; the V2+SGD audit (round 4) reached 99.95 % in 200 epochs. The per-layer gradient ratio at fmnist/100L/BN dropped from 2.9×10⁴ (Adam, smoke3) to 1.0×10² (SGD, audit4) — a 300× reduction that lets SGD actually make progress even though it still didn't reach the bar. The mechanism is precisely the one V2's theory implies — V2 + plain SGD is the natural pairing, not V2 + Adam.

The round-4 trajectory plot is at reports/figures/final_v2_audit4_bn_sgd.png.

8.2 Mechanism — V2 has a hard depth ceiling at L≈30 under no-clipping recipes

V2's per-layer weight std follows the schedule

s_l = s* · r^{η·(l − (L+1)/2)},   r = √((π−1)/π) ≈ 0.826,   s* = (π/(π−1))^{1/4} ≈ 1.10

so the layer-1-to-layer-L std ratio is r^{−η·(L−1)}, which is exponential in L. At η=0.5:

  • L=30: 16× — manageable. V2 produces a forward pass with peak A_RMS ≈ 6, and gradient max/min ratio (end of training) of 11–76× — lower than He's 16–130×. V2 delivers its design promise here.
  • L=50: 109× — marginal. Smoke shows initial learning (54 % peak on fmnist), but the audit recipe (SGD onecycle lr=0.01 ramping up, or Adam adaptive) breaks past the per-layer stability margin within tens of epochs.
  • L=100: 13,144× — catastrophic. V2's layer-1 std at L=100 is ~126× He's (multiplier r^{-24.75} ≈ 113), producing per-layer activation amplification that exceeds float32's 3.4×10³⁸ ceiling at layer ~11. No gradient is ever computed because the loss is NaN before backward.

8.3 BN partially helps but does not lift the ceiling

Adding BN at L=100 (the option B test, recovery3 recipe minus clipping) cures the forward-pass overflow — per-layer activation RMS stays at ≈ 0.7 instead of inf. But BN normalization is in the forward direction only; the per-layer gradient profile still inherits V2's per-layer weight differential because ∂C/∂W^(l) ∝ rms(A^{l−1}) · rms(Δ^l) and the local upstream-error chain magnitudes still vary by V2's per-layer schedule. Per-batch logging at ep 1 b 0 shows the per-layer gradient ratio is 5.7×10⁴ for cifar10/100L/BN and 2.9×10⁴ for fmnist/100L/BN. Adam's per-parameter adaptive scaling cannot condition this ratio in 20 epochs (fmnist stayed at chance; cifar10 spiked loss at ep 20 when LR warmup completed). The smoke3 trajectory is in reports/figures/final_v2_smoke3_100L_bn.png.

8.4 Lowering η degenerates V2 toward V1

Dropping to η=0.1 survives the L=100 forward pass (peak A_RMS ≈ 4.6×10⁸, within float32) but leaves the per-layer gradient ratio at 5×10⁷ at ep 1 b 0 — still SGD-destabilizing. By ep 1 b 2, loss has exploded to 10²² (the explosion-guard aborts). Pushing η further toward 0 reduces V2 to V1 (uniform per-layer std), which defeats the experimental purpose of testing the η-scheme. We did not pursue this branch.

8.5 Implication for the thesis

V2 (row_centered_layer_balanced_product_base, η=0.5) trains 4 / 12 architectures in the audit — all at depth 30, both datasets, both BN states. Within its working depth range, V2 produces measurably more uniform per-layer gradient profiles than He (V2 max/min ratio = 11–76× vs He = 16–130× across the 4 passing architectures), confirming the η-scheme's analytical claim that gradient balance is improved by per-layer variance redistribution.

Beyond L=30, V2 fails through one of three depth-dependent mechanisms: (a) float32 forward overflow at L=100 NoBN; (b) Adam double-preconditioning at L=50/100 BN; (c) onecycle / momentum-SGD instability past the per-layer scale margin at L=50 NoBN. Adding BN cures (a) but not (b). Lowering η toward 0 degenerates V2 to V1.

The unsolved L=100/BN regime affects both He (recovery 3: peak 21–47 %, drifted) and V2 (smoke3: stuck at chance) — it is a depth-100 / optimizer-recipe problem, not an initializer-specific failure. Conclusion: V2 is best framed as a moderate-depth initializer (L ≤ 30) within the depth regime where its per-layer scaling stays within float32 + SGD-friendly margins. Beyond L=30, V2 requires either BN + a substantially better optimizer (which we have not identified) or a complementary per-layer LR scheme (option C in the followup plan, untested), or it degenerates back to V1.

Full V2 audit trajectories, per-layer gradient profiles, and the comprehensive 12-row scoreboard with mechanism notes are in notebooks/13_final_results.ipynb Part 4 (Sections 19.1 – 19.7).
